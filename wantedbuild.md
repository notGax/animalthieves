We’re building a 1–4 player physics based co-op heist game in Godot. Players are slimes who raid stores, steal physical merchandise, fight animal security guards and defeat each store’s boss to progress farther down the road.

Rob stores, defeat their animal bosses, recover production blueprints and build an underground criminal business with your trader partner.

The world and lobby

The lobby is a long road lined with stores. A manhole in the middle leads to the gang’s underground base.

Stores on either side of the road act as playable levels.
Stores become more dangerous farther down the road.
Each store has its own animal species, products, security systems and boss.
Defeating a store’s boss unlocks access to the next section of the road.
The final store is guarded by a mecha dragon controlled by a lizard inside it.

The underground base contains the trader, production machines, unlocked blueprints and equipment shop.

The main loop
Collect income generated by the underground machines.
Equip items purchased from the trader.
Enter a store from the road.
Steal merchandise and discover environmental interactions.
Avoid or fight guards as security escalates.
Reach and defeat the store’s boss guard.
Escape with the loot and boss rewards.
Unlock the next store.
Install or upgrade the recovered blueprint.
Reinvest the income into equipment, machines and transformations.

Players can revisit earlier stores to search for missed rewards, improve blueprint levels or attempt to obtain their bosses’ transformation items.

Boss guards

Every level has a boss guard representing that store’s animal species.

Defeating the boss:

Unlocks the next level.
Awards a blueprint or recipe.
Upgrades that blueprint when the boss is defeated again.
Has a chance to drop the boss’s species transformation item.
Provides valuable loot and materials.

Blueprint progression is guaranteed. Transformation drops remain exciting because they are chance based.

Underground production business

The trader is the gang’s partner. They install machines beneath the road that automatically produce and sell items from recovered blueprints.

Players begin with one machine slot. They can:

Buy additional machines from the trader.
Install one blueprint in each machine.
Replace an installed blueprint with another.
Upgrade blueprints by defeating their associated bosses again.
Choose between several cheap products or fewer valuable products.

Machines visibly change as their installed blueprints improve. A rotten-cheese machine might begin as a leaking barrel and eventually become a loud, dangerous industrial cheese reactor.

Production continues while players are actively playing missions. Returning from a robbery provides income from the machines alongside the loot carried home.

Because machine slots are limited, players must choose what their underground business produces. Passive production is the main stable income source, while stolen loot and boss rewards provide immediate bonuses and unlocks.

Transformations

Players normally exist as slimes. Animal forms come from rare transformation items dropped by bosses.

Every boss has a chance to drop its species transformation item.
The item is not guaranteed, encouraging level replays.
When any player obtains it, that transformation becomes available from the trader for everyone else.
Each animal transformation can be upgraded.
All players can eventually access every transformation.
Duplicate animal forms are allowed.

Activating an animal form begins a 120-second species-change cooldown. The player can return to slime form during this time but cannot transform into another animal species until the cooldown ends.

Transformations provide abilities rather than permanent classes. For example, a rat form could:

Enter rat tunnels.
Chew through cables, wooden barriers and packaging.
Smell nearby cheese and hidden food.
Communicate with or briefly deceive ordinary rat guards.
Carry more after upgrading into a larger rat.
Food, guards and bribery

Food no longer determines the player’s species. It remains a flexible resource with several uses:

Sell it as loot.
Give it to a matching guard as a bribe.
Eat it for a temporary effect.
Feed it to teammates or guards.
Use it as bait or a distraction.
Process it using store appliances.

Different foods can still give special effects. Rotten cheese might increase speed while causing the player to leave a smell trail. Moonshine might increase strength and reduce control. Effects apply to guards too, allowing players to poison, strengthen, distract or accidentally enrage them.

First level: the rat store

The first playable level is a dingy roadside store operated and guarded by rats.

It sells:

Smelly rotten cheese.
Jail-made moonshine.
Suspicious canned food.
Stolen household products.
Rat traps repackaged as “security equipment.”
Cheap valuables hidden behind the counter.

The store should look dirty, damp and badly repaired, with holes in the walls, exposed pipes, broken refrigeration, low shelves and a basement distillery.

Basic first-level experience

New players only need to:

Grab products.
Carry them outside.
Avoid ordinary rat guards.
Reach the back of the store.
Defeat the Big Rat boss.

Later attempts reveal more possibilities:

Rotten cheese distracts rat guards.
Moonshine can be sold, consumed or used as fuel.
Rat holes provide shortcuts after unlocking rat form.
Shopping carts can transport large amounts of loot.
Leaking alcohol can be ignited.
Cheese smell can lure several guards into one place.
Players can sabotage the distillery to weaken the boss or create a chaotic escape route.
Big Rat boss

The boss is an enormous chef rat who protects the store’s recipe and production operation.

Its fight can revolve around the same systems used throughout the level:

It follows strong cheese smells.
It drinks moonshine and becomes stronger but less accurate.
Players can redirect its charges into shelves and walls.
Spilled liquid changes its movement.
The environment contains improvised cooking weapons.
Damaging the distillery changes the arena.

The prototype can use Remy as the internal joke or reference. A public release should use an original chef-rat character rather than the actual Ratatouille character, since Remy belongs to Disney/Pixar.

Defeating the boss:

Unlocks the next store on the road.
Awards the Rotten Cheese Recipe.
Improves that recipe on later victories.
Has a chance to drop the Rat Transformation Item.
Trader equipment

The trader’s shop currently sells:

Grappling hook: reach shelves, pull objects and drag teammates.
Shopping cart: transport several items or players.
Mini truck: carry large loot and serve as an escape vehicle.
Racecar: scout, distract guards or transport tiny objects.
Water gun: heals teammates but cannot heal its user.
Slime crystal: revives a defeated teammate.
Jetpack: provides temporary vertical movement.

All equipment should interact with players and the environment. Grappling hooks can pull loot or create disasters; shopping carts can roll away; the racecar can carry bribes; the water gun can affect store machinery or wash away scent trails.

Presentation

Slime characters use expressive eye and mouth animation similar to the character responsiveness in R.E.P.O. and PEAK.

Eyes follow nearby players, hands, loot and threats.
Characters look toward whatever they are holding.
Mouths react to falling, damage, valuable discoveries and transformations.
Animal forms retain the player’s recognizable eyes and mouth.
Looking directly at another player creates natural silent comedy.

The game’s current identity is:

A gang of slimes robs animal-run stores, steals the recipes behind their businesses and turns those recipes into an expanding underground production empire. Bosses unlock new stores, rare transformations change how robberies can be approached, and almost every useful object can become loot, equipment, a bribe or the cause of the group’s next disaster.
