# Visual Reference Analysis

This analysis uses official in-game screenshots stored under `reference-screenshots/`. The goal is to identify reusable visual principles, not reproduce any one game's assets or signature look.

## Primary References

### R.E.P.O.

- Best reference for the central contrast: extremely simple, readable characters inside dark and threatening spaces.
- Characters use compact silhouettes, oversized luminous faces, and very little surface detail.
- Warm pools of light and saturated danger colors make interactions legible without making the whole environment bright.
- Useful contribution: face readability, character/world contrast, selective lighting, physical-object staging.

### How to Fish

- Best reference for deliberately crude low-poly construction, animal-like characters, and visible first-person equipment.
- Angular trees, simple buildings, flat-shaded creatures, and restrained textures make the world inexpensive to build but still spatially coherent.
- Useful contribution: polygon language, production feasibility, first-person hands/tools, absurd scale contrast.

### PEAK

- Best reference for strong silhouettes, clear color grouping, and readable teammates at long distances.
- Environments are stylized but atmospheric; large forms carry the scene while small props are simplified.
- Useful contribution: silhouette discipline, palette separation, readable traversal spaces, charming character proportions.

### Schedule I

- Best reference for a mundane criminal world made from straightforward geometry and simple materials.
- Buildings and interiors feel like recognizable places without dense asset complexity.
- Useful contribution: roadside world construction, shop readability, underground production spaces, restrained urban grime.

## Secondary References

### Lethal Company

- Useful for fog, darkness, industrial silhouettes, and keeping environmental detail subordinate to danger and navigation.
- Its nearly anonymous suited characters are less useful for this game's expressive slime identity.

### Content Warning

- Useful for the aggressive separation between safe, colorful spaces and dangerous monochrome or red-lit spaces.
- Its stark lighting shifts are useful; its exact character construction should not be borrowed.

### WEBFISHING

- Useful for animal shorthand, readable facial marks, warm social spaces, and unapologetically simple geometry.
- Its flat, paper-like character treatment is probably too graphic for a physics-heavy first-person heist game.

### Human: Fall Flat

- Useful for simple riggable bodies, costume modularity, and animation-led personality.
- Its clean white forms and dreamlike world are too soft and sterile for the proposed stores.

### Gang Beasts

- Useful for chunky gelatinous silhouettes, costumes, slapstick posing, and saturated player differentiation.
- Its glossy toy-like finish and consistently bright environments would weaken the desired world/character contrast.

### Murky Divers

- Useful for visible first-person hands, cramped machinery, murky color casts, and grotesque threats.
- Its heavy post-processing and visual noise should be used sparingly because this game needs merchandise to remain readable.

### PICO PARK

- Useful only as an extreme lesson in instant silhouette and color identification across multiple players.
- Its 2D minimalism is not a direct modeling reference.

## Proposed Original Synthesis

- **Characters:** extremely simple, chunky, low-poly bodies with large graphic faces and animation-driven expression.
- **World:** recognizable roadside architecture with moderate geometric detail, worn materials, strong prop silhouettes, and selective grime.
- **Lighting:** moody overall exposure with localized pools of dirty fluorescent, warm practical, emergency-red, and production-machine light.
- **Materials:** restrained texture resolution, broad color blocks, visible wear only where it communicates use or danger.
- **Readability rule:** teammates, guards, loot, hazards, and exits must remain identifiable before decorative details are added.
- **Originality rule:** borrow production principles and contrasts, never another game's character anatomy, signature face, costume, or exact palette.
